# B1: the water chemistry against the source platform, findings for the owner

Owner amendment B1 (2026-11-05): the Rust core is tested three ways, against the source platform's engine and our
Python, and every disagreement is a finding the owner decides before the core follows either side. This is the
first report (chemcore step S3). **Decided (owner amendment B5, 2026-11-06): all seven findings for ours, the
literature-correct side; golden v1 stays the reference, no v2.** The Rust core is held to golden v1. The source's
defects are listed for the source team at the end and are not copied.

## How it was compared

- **Source:** aquaculture_platform commit `352aa2a8a94a178dd6543de6b741c520f5a1d6f3`, the files of
  `libs/aquaculture-engines/src/water-chemistry/` and `web/shared-ui/src/water-chemistry/{compute,types}.ts`

  run in the source's checkout.

- **Corpus:** 1 245 temperature and salinity pairs (every constant), 1 920 carbonate waters (T 0 to 40 °C, S 0 to
  45 ‰, pH 4 to 12, alkalinity 1 to 800 mg/L), 106 complete waters through the calculator's whole calculation, 73
  charts (every line checked point by point: ours evaluated at the source's own DIC), 2 394 recipe cases and 102

- **The test stays:** `farm/tests/test_b1.py` fails on any disagreement no finding covers, on a finding whose

Every constant on every pH scale (K1, K2, Kw, KB, KNH₄, KH₂S, KS, KF, both Ksp, the scale factors free/SWS/total,

source's own downstream functions fed our DIC. **The links agree:** the same intermediate values flow into the same
outputs on both sides; every disagreement below has one cause, not a chain.

## Findings (20 quantities disagree; each covered once)

### B1-01 · The molar mass of CO₂: 44.0096 (source) against 44.0095 (ours)

- **Where:** `water-quality.ts` `co2MmToMg` / `co2MgToMm` multiply by 44.0096 (and the chart's CO₂ zone uses 44.010,
  WQ-35); ours `chemistry.MG_CO2_PER_MMOL = 44.0095`.
- **Disagreeing:** CO₂ in mg/L (all 106 waters, relative 2.27e-6), the target's CO₂ (72), the CO₂ border pH (106 and
  3 × 1 920, up to 9.9e-7 pH), the safe zone's CO₂ corners (142, up to 2.4e-6 mmol/L).
- **Example:** 12 °C, S 0, pH 4.5, 20 mg/L: CO₂ ours 1674.61711 mg/L, source 1674.62092 mg/L. A border, 23.4 °C,
  S 0, 47.2 mg/L, CO₂ limit 15 mg/L: ours pH 6.8026536, source 6.8026546.
- **Cause, tested:** our CO₂ × 44.0096 / 44.0095 equals the source's to 1e-12; our border solved for 15/44.0096 equals
  the source's to 1e-8.
- **Literature:** CO₂ = C + 2 O. With the IUPAC standard atomic weights of 2007 (C 12.0107, O 15.9994) it is
  44.0095 g/mol; with the abridged values of 2013 (C 12.011, O 15.999) 44.009. 44.0096 is neither (it is the value
  of the R CarbCalc code the source was ported from). **Ours is right;** the effect (2 ppm, 1e-6 pH) is far below any
  measurement.
- **Decided:** ours, 44.0095 (B5).

### B1-02 · A CO₂ a DIC cannot hold: the source answers pH 0, ours "no pH"

- **Where:** `water-quality.ts` `calcPhForCritCO2` returns its search's lower end (`minPH`, 0) when the CO₂ asked for
  is at or above the DIC; ours `Equilibria.ph_where_co2_at_dic` returns None.
- **Disagreeing:** 105 of 1 920 (every case where 0.3 mmol/L CO₂ ≥ the DIC). **Example:** 0 °C, S 0, pH 7, 10 mg/L
  (DIC 0.24 mmol/L): ours None, source 0.
- **Cause, tested:** in every case ours is None and the source's answer is a search end (0 or 14).
- **Literature:** CO₂ = DIC α0 and α0 < 1 at every pH, so no pH gives a CO₂ above the DIC. **Ours is right;** a pH of 0
  would be a false border. (The source's chart does not call this function; its zone uses another one, which agrees.)
- **Decided:** ours, no pH (B5).

### B1-03 · Borders beyond the source's search range are clamped (WQ-34)

- **Where:** `ammonia-calc.ts` `criticalPHforNH3` bisects between pH 4 and 12, so a border above 12 comes out 12;
  `deffeyes-data.ts` `criticalPHforH2SPHChartDomain` returns 3.0 (4 − 1) below pH 4 and 13.5 above 12.5;
  `generateH2SToxicZone` draws a border above 12 at 12. Ours solves the border in closed form wherever it is.
- **Disagreeing:** the NH₃ border (1 water), the NH₃ share at the border, the H₂S border (2), the chart's NH₃ line (10
  points), H₂S line (20), whether the H₂S zone is drawn (1) and the safe zone's NH₃ side (1).
- **Examples:** 12 °C, S 1, pH 7, 80 mg/L, TAN 0.01031 mg/L against an NH₃-N limit of 0.0103: NH₃ border ours pH
  12.730, source 12.000 (NH₃ share at the border ours 99.90 %, source 99.48 %); sulfide 10 µg/L as S against an H₂S
  limit of 99.999 % of it: ours pH 2.116, source 3 (a sentinel printed as "Toxic H₂S pH Border 3.000"); sulfide
  20 000 µg/L against 0.01 µg/L: the H₂S border is above pH 12.5 (ours 13.44), and one point of the H₂S line, ours
  161.3 meq/L on the real border's isoline, source 47.2 meq/L on the pH 12 isoline it draws instead.
- **Cause, tested:** every differing border is beyond the source's range (NH₃ above 12; H₂S below 4 or above 12),
  and the source's value is the range's end or its sentinel.
- **Literature:** the border is a property of the water (Emerson et al. 1975; Millero 1995 for NH₄⁺; Millero et al.
  1988 for H₂S), not of a search range. **Ours is right;** the statuses agree on every water of the corpus (both are
  "ok" for the clamped cases), so no status shown today differs.
- **Decided:** ours, the real border (B5).

### B1-04 · Reagent molar masses: the source's rounded values against ours from the atomic weights

- **Where:** `reagents.ts` `REAGENTS[].mw` (NaHCO₃ 84.007, Na₂CO₃ 105.988, NaOH 39.997, CaCO₃ 100.087, Ca(OH)₂
  74.093, CaO 56.077, CO₂ 44.010, HCl 36.461); ours `dosing.molar_mass` from the IUPAC 2007 atomic weights (NaHCO₃
  84.00661, CO₂ 44.0095, ...).
- **Disagreeing:** every dose of every recipe (1 548 doses, relative up to 1.1e-5) and the simulator's DIC and pH
  after a dose (up to 5e-6 pH).
- **Example:** 8 °C, S 0, pH 6.6, 30 mg/L to pH 6.8 and 150 mg/L, 37.5 m³, NaHCO₃: ours 5 589.678 g, source 5 589.741 g.
- **Cause, tested:** our grams × the source's molar mass / ours equal the source's to 1e-11.
- **Literature:** both agree with the atomic weights to their printed digits; ours carries more digits and one CO₂
  value throughout. The difference (0.001 %) is far below any scale. **Neither is wrong; ours is the consistent one.**
- **Decided:** ours (B5).

### B1-05 · A base with CO₂ stripping gives no recipe in the source (WQ-40)

- **Disagreeing:** 9 of 2 394 recipe cases: NaOH, Ca(OH)₂ or CaO with "De-gas CO₂", target pH 8.3 and 80 mg/L from
  12 °C, S 1, pH 7, 80 mg/L: ours one recipe, source none.
- **Cause, tested:** every such case is one of those three bases with stripping; the source's slope intersection
  computes (−∞)/(−∞) = NaN and drops the pair.
- **Literature:** a 2 × 2 linear system on the two direction vectors (Cramer's rule) has the solution; **ours is right.**
- **Decided:** ours (B5).

### B1-06 · The source's simulator clamps DIC and alkalinity at 0 (WQ-41)

- **Disagreeing:** the simulator's DIC (48 of 102) and pH (60 of 102). **Examples:** 4 kg HCl in 1 m³ of 8 °C, S 0,
  pH 6.6, 30 mg/L water: ours pH 0.96 (108 mmol/L of acid left over), source pH 4.73 (alkalinity clamped at 0);
  500 g of CO₂ stripped from 1 m³: ours "no water" (DIC −9.4 mmol/L, the simulation stops and says so), source DIC 0
  and pH NaN. The remaining small differences are B1-04's molar masses.
- **Cause, tested:** every differing DIC is ours below 0 against the source's 0, or within 1e-4 relative.
- **Literature:** acid past the alkalinity is mineral acidity, a negative alkalinity (Stumm & Morgan, Aquatic
  Chemistry); a clamp hides an overdose. **Ours is right.**
- **Decided:** ours (B5).

### B1-07 · The source's safe zone ignores H₂S, can cross itself, and vanishes for the safest water (WQ-30)

- **Disagreeing:** whether the safe zone is drawn, 35 of 73 charts. **Example:** 21.5 °C, S 0.2, pH 8.12, 574 mg/L,
  TAN 1.53 mg/L, sulfide 48.9 µg/L as S, limits NH₃-N 0.0103, CO₂ 15, H₂S 2, band 50 to 150 mg/L: ours none (the H₂S
  border, pH 8.39, is above the NH₃ border, pH 7.21: no pH is inside both), source a quadrilateral inside the H₂S
  toxic region.
- **Cause, tested:** where only the source draws, either ours without the sulfide draws it (H₂S narrows it away) or
  the source's quadrilateral is inverted (its CO₂ corner left of its NH₃ corner: no pH is inside both limits); where
  only ours draws, the TAN cannot reach the NH₃ limit and the source's zone vanishes.
- **Literature:** a zone is safe only inside every limit (US EPA 1986 for H₂S and NH₃). **Ours is right.**
- **Decided:** ours (B5).

# I2: fresh water has no ionic strength in the model (a note for the owner)

This is a known scientific limitation, reported as the owner asked (B4); the port keeps it faithfully. Owner
amendment B5 schedules its correction as its own step right after the port (after S10): golden v3, side by side with v1
on fresh, hard and brackish waters, shown to the owner before any switch.

**What the code does.** The ionic strength comes from the salinity alone, I = 19.924 S / (1000 − 1.005 S)
(`chemistry._ionic_strength`), and at S = 0 every scale factor and the H⁺ activity coefficient are exactly 1
(`_free_to_sws`, `_free_to_total`, `_activity_coefficient_h`). The carbonic acid constants also move with √S
(Millero et al. 2006). The source does the same.

**Why it matters in fresh water.** A fresh water is not ideal: a hard water (Ca 2, Mg 0.5, Na 1 mmol/L with its
bicarbonate and chloride) has an ionic strength of about 0.01, where the Davies equation gives log γH⁺ ≈ −0.044. The
model treats it as S = 0 and so as ideal, while the same water with a salinity of 0.1 ‰ entered (or derived from a
conductivity sensor) is treated as slightly saline. Measured on today's code at 12 °C, 80 mg/L, pH 7:

| Salinity (‰) | pH offset (free − NBS) | pK1 (free) | CO₂ (mg/L) | NH₃ share at pH 7.5 (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.0000 | 6.4456 | 19.617 | 0.6823 |
| 0.01 | −0.0072 | 6.4321 | 19.333 | 0.6728 |
| 0.1 | −0.0207 | 6.4049 | 18.729 | 0.6536 |
| 0.2 | −0.0278 | 6.3895 | 18.372 | 0.6427 |
| 0.5 | −0.0399 | 6.3609 | 17.683 | 0.6229 |

So a salinity that flickers between 0 and 0.1 ‰ (a conductivity-derived value near fresh water) moves the CO₂ by
0.9 mg/L (4.5 %) and the NH₃ share by 4 %, and the water's point and borders by 0.01 to 0.03 pH, with nothing in the
water having changed; and a hard fresh water entered as S 0 is computed without the 0.04 pH its ionic strength
would give. University tanks are mostly fresh water, so this is the most visible of the model's limits.

**What a correction would be (later, owner-approved):** take the ionic strength of fresh water from what is measured
(conductivity, I ≈ 1.6e-5 × κ in µS/cm, Russell 1976; or hardness and alkalinity) and use an activity model valid
there (Davies) for the NBS conversion, keeping the seawater path for S ≥ 5. It changes results, so it would be golden
v3, compared with v1 and shown to the owner before any switch.

**Built as golden v3, not switched (2026-11-09).** The correction above is in the core (`chemcore/core/src/fresh.rs`,
`native.equilibria_v3`), recorded as `chemcore/golden/v3` and held by `farm/tests/test_golden_v3.py`; the application
still reads v1. The side-by-side numbers for fresh, hard and brackish waters, and the choice of the boundary (5 ‰ as
planned, or 1 ‰, where v3 meets v1 within 1 % on salinity alone), are in `chemcore/findings/I2-comparison.md`.
