## Findings (20 quantities disagree; each covered once)

### B1-01 · The molar mass of CO₂: 44.0096 (source) against 44.0095 (ours)

- **Where:** `water-quality.ts` `co2MmToMg` / `co2MgToMm` multiply by 44.0096 (and the chart's CO₂ zone uses 44.010,
  WQ-35); ours `chemistry.MG_CO2_PER_MMOL = 44.0095`.
- **Disagreeing:** CO₂ in mg/L (all 106 waters, relative 2.27e-6), the target's CO₂ (72), the CO₂ border pH (106 and
  3 × 1 920, up to 9.9e-7 pH), the safe zone's CO₂ corners (142, up to 2.4e-6 mmol/L).
- **Example:** 12 °C, S 0, pH 4.5, 20 mg/L: CO₂ ours 1674.61711 mg/L, source 1674.62092 mg/L. A border, 23.4 °C,
  S 0, 47.2 mg/L, CO₂ limit 15 mg/L: ours pH 6.8026536, source 6.8026546.
- **Cause, tested:** our CO₂ × 44.0096 / 44.0095 equals the source's to 1e-12; our border solved for 15/44.0096 equals
  the source's to 1e-8.
- **Literature:** CO₂ = C + 2 O. With the IUPAC standard atomic weights of 2007 (C 12.0107, O 15.9994) it is
  44.0095 g/mol; with the abridged values of 2013 (C 12.011, O 15.999) 44.009. 44.0096 is neither (it is the value
  of the R CarbCalc code the source was ported from). **Ours is right;** the effect (2 ppm, 1e-6 pH) is far below any
  measurement.
- **Decided:** ours, 44.0095 (B5).

### B1-02 · A CO₂ a DIC cannot hold: the source answers pH 0, ours "no pH"

# I2: fresh water has no ionic strength in the model (a note for the owner)

This is a known scientific limitation, reported as the owner asked (B4); the port keeps it faithfully. Owner
amendment B5 schedules its correction as its own step right after the port (after S10): golden v3, side by side with v1
on fresh, hard and brackish waters, shown to the owner before any switch.

**What the code does.** The ionic strength comes from the salinity alone, I = 19.924 S / (1000 − 1.005 S)
(`chemistry._ionic_strength`), and at S = 0 every scale factor and the H⁺ activity coefficient are exactly 1
(`_free_to_sws`, `_free_to_total`, `_activity_coefficient_h`). The carbonic acid constants also move with √S
(Millero et al. 2006). The source does the same.

**Why it matters in fresh water.** A fresh water is not ideal: a hard water (Ca 2, Mg 0.5, Na 1 mmol/L with its
bicarbonate and chloride) has an ionic strength of about 0.01, where the Davies equation gives log γH⁺ ≈ −0.044. The
model treats it as S = 0 and so as ideal, while the same water with a salinity of 0.1 ‰ entered (or derived from a
conductivity sensor) is treated as slightly saline. Measured on today's code at 12 °C, 80 mg/L, pH 7:

| Salinity (‰) | pH offset (free − NBS) | pK1 (free) | CO₂ (mg/L) | NH₃ share at pH 7.5 (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.0000 | 6.4456 | 19.617 | 0.6823 |
| 0.01 | −0.0072 | 6.4321 | 19.333 | 0.6728 |
| 0.1 | −0.0207 | 6.4049 | 18.729 | 0.6536 |
| 0.2 | −0.0278 | 6.3895 | 18.372 | 0.6427 |
| 0.5 | −0.0399 | 6.3609 | 17.683 | 0.6229 |

So a salinity that flickers between 0 and 0.1 ‰ (a conductivity-derived value near fresh water) moves the CO₂ by
0.9 mg/L (4.5 %) and the NH₃ share by 4 %, and the water's point and borders by 0.01 to 0.03 pH, with nothing in the
water having changed; and a hard fresh water entered as S 0 is computed without the 0.04 pH its ionic strength
would give. University tanks are mostly fresh water, so this is the most visible of the model's limits.

**What a correction would be (later, owner-approved):** take the ionic strength of fresh water from what is measured
(conductivity, I ≈ 1.6e-5 × κ in µS/cm, Russell 1976; or hardness and alkalinity) and use an activity model valid
there (Davies) for the NBS conversion, keeping the seawater path for S ≥ 5. It changes results, so it would be golden
v3, compared with v1 and shown to the owner before any switch.

**Switched: formula 3 (owner, 2026-11-09).** The correction above is in the core (`chemcore/core/src/fresh.rs`) and is
what the application works out (`FORMULA_VERSION` 3; `chemistry.water_equilibria`). Up to 1 ‰ a water's ionic strength
comes from what is measured: the conductivity compensated to 25 °C (I = 1.6e-5 κ, Russell 1976) else the salinity,
never below the lower bound of the measured ions (the hardness with the alkalinity, else the alkalinity alone), 0 with
nothing measured (formula 1's ideal water, the safe side); its constants are the thermodynamic ones made concentration
constants with Davies activity coefficients. From 5 ‰ the seawater constants of formula 1, with the NBS conversion's A
of the temperature (I1, below). Between 1 and 5 ‰ each constant is the geometric blend of the two, the sea's weight
linear in the salinity: Millero et al. (2006) measured K1 and K2 from S 1, Dickson's (1990) KSO₄ and KB hold from S 5.
The old 5 ‰ boundary jumped up to 25 % in CO₂ and 14.5 % in the NH₃ share (a hard water); the band has no jump, its
steepest change 1.4 % per 0.1 ‰. The conductivity and the hardness are water parameters (cross review F8); the
measured jump table, the choice and the before and after numbers are in `chemcore/findings/I2-comparison.md` (Turkish,
for the owner). Formula 1 is still worked out exactly when asked for by name (golden v1, `formula=1`), never by the
application.

# I1: the Debye–Hückel A had a fixed dielectric constant (closed in formula 3)

**What formula 1 does.** The Debye–Hückel A of the H⁺ activity coefficient (Zeebe & Wolf-Gladrow 2001's Davies-type
equation, the NBS-to-seawater-scale conversion) is 1.82e6 (ε T)^−1.5 with ε fixed at 79, water's dielectric constant
near 25 °C (`chemistry::debye_huckel_a_v1`). Water's ε rises as it cools (87.74 at 0 °C, Malmberg & Maryott 1956), so
A was too large at farm temperatures: 0.5383 at 12 °C against 0.5004 (+7.5 %), 0.5679 at 2 °C against 0.4932 (+15 %),
and 0.5035 at 25 °C against 0.5108 (−1.4 %). Golden v3 used the same A for Davies until the cross review (F2).

**Formula 3.** A = 1.824812e6 √ρ (ε T)^−3/2 (log₁₀, molal; the prefactor (1/ln 10) √(2π N_A) (e²/(4π ε₀ k))^3/2 worked
out with the CODATA 2018 constants; Hamer 1968 prints 1.824829238e6, Nordstrom et al. 1984 1.824814e6), with ε of
Malmberg & Maryott (1956, J. Res. NBS 56: 1, eq. 5, measured 0.1 to 99 °C) and ρ of Kell (1975, J. Chem. Eng. Data
20: 97; the coefficients as reproduced in J. Res. NIST 97: 335 (1992), whose denominator misprints 16.897850e-3 for
16.879850e-3; Kell's own paper was not read). Checked (`chemcore/core/tests/fresh.rs`, `farm/tests/test_golden_v3.py`):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 87.740 | 87.740 | 0.4918 | 0.4918 | 0.4912 | 0.5742 |
| 5 | 85.763 | 85.763 | 0.4953 | 0.4953 | — | 0.5587 |
| 10 | 83.832 | 83.832 | 0.4989 | 0.4989 | 0.4976 | 0.5440 |

The core meets Hamer's table within 0.03 % (Hamer took ε from Malmberg & Maryott too) and the USGS table, whose ε is
Uematsu & Franck's (1980), within 0.3 %. Davies with this A meets Davies with Hamer's tabulated A within 0.1 % in γ at
2, 5 and 12 °C (I 0.001 to 0.1, z 1 and 2).

**Who uses A.** Two places, both changed in formula 3: Davies in the fresh-water path, and the seawater path's NBS
conversion (`chemistry::activity_coefficient_h_with`). The latter moves the sea path's pH offset by up to +0.026 at
0 °C and −0.002 at 25 °C. Against CO2SYS's NBS convention (the apparent H⁺ activity coefficient of Takahashi et al.
1982, fitted for S 20 to 40; as seacarb and CO2SYS code it), formula 1's offset at 35 ‰ differed by −0.073 at 0 °C,
−0.058 at 5 °C, +0.004 at 25 °C and +0.051 at 40 °C; formula 3's by −0.048, −0.038, +0.001 and +0.034: closer at every
temperature from 10 ‰ up (at 5 ‰, outside Takahashi's fit, it moves away, by 0.019 at 0 °C). In sea water at 2 °C this shows up
to 5.4 % less CO₂ and 5.6 % more NH₃ than formula 1 (the before-and-after table in the comparison).

# Cross review of formula 3 (2026-11-09): what changed

**F1, the basis NH₃ and CO₂ are judged on (owner decision, safety with live fish).** The published NH₃ limits were set
on Emerson et al. (1975)'s ideal fraction, every activity 1 (Piper et al. 1982's 0.0125 mg/L NH₃; US EPA 1985), and
formula 3's activity-corrected NH₃ is lower in an ion-rich water: compared with limits of the ideal basis it would
understate. Every NH₃ judgement, alarm, status, border, safe TAN and chart therefore takes the larger of the share on the
limits' basis (`fresh::limits_basis`: the ideal water up to 1 ‰, formula 1's seawater constants from 5 ‰, blended
between as formula 3 blends) and the corrected share (`Equilibria::nh3_share`; the corrected one is
`nh3_share_corrected`, shown as information). The larger share is the larger K·10^offset at every pH, so one basis
judges a water at every pH (`nh3_basis`). The same rule holds for CO₂ (`co2_judged`, `ph_where_co2`): the salmonid CO₂
guidelines rest partly on free CO₂ worked out from pH and alkalinity with every activity 1 (Standard Methods 4500-CO₂ D,
the equation the calculator is checked against, `test_free_co2_follows_standard_methods`) and partly on probes, which
read the true concentration formula 3 approximates; the basis of each study was not verified (Fivelstad et al. 1999,
Aquaculture 178: 171, report CO₂ as a partial pressure; their method sections were not read), so the larger is taken.

| 12 °C, 80 mg/L, pH 7.5 | NH₃ corrected against judged | CO₂ corrected against judged |
|---|---|---|
| 500 µS/cm | −8.7 % | −8.8 % |
| 1000 µS/cm | −11.6 % | −11.7 % |
| 3000 µS/cm | −17.2 % | −17.5 % |

In fresh water the limits' basis is always the larger (γ₁ < 1), so formula 3 judges exactly as the ideal water does.
In cold sea water formula 3's own share is the larger (+5.5 % NH₃ at 2 °C, 35 ‰; F2) and judges; at 25 °C formula 1's.
Up to 1 ‰ the basis is the ideal water even where formula 1 took seawater constants (0 < S ≤ 1), the stricter: the
calculator's example (12 °C, 1 ‰) now has its NH₃ border at 7.986 (corrected 8.045, formula 1 8.040) and its CO₂ at
19.62 mg/L (corrected 17.12, formula 1 16.94). Golden v3 was re-recorded with both values and the basis; golden v1 is
unchanged (the four new analysis fields follow from it for formula 1, `golden_parity.ADDED`).
